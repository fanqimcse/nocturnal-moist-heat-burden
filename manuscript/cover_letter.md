# Cover letter — Nature Climate Change (initial submission)

Manuscript title: *Night-time moist heat driven by humidity, not temperature alone, is crossing
physiologically anchored survival thresholds — with mortality risk attaching to the crossing event
itself*

Dear Editors,

We submit the above Article for consideration in *Nature Climate Change*.

**The problem.** Humid-heat impact assessment currently rests on daytime, intensity-based metrics
anchored to a 35 °C wet-bulb limit that laboratory physiology has now superseded (Vanos et al.
2023; Meade et al. 2025). Whether night-time hours are crossing physiologically anchored
thresholds — and what that means for mortality — has not been quantified globally.

**What we show.**
1. Using sub-hourly data from 176 stations (1976–2023), night-time hours above the 21.9 °C
   older-adult survivability bound rose by **+42.8 h per decade**; warming alone explains none of
   the rise (humidity requirement +106%).
2. Optimal-fingerprint analysis detects the anthropogenic contribution (NAT rejected) and shows
   CMIP6 overstates the observed rise 2.3–2.9×; all projections are observation-constrained.
3. In 225 European regions (77,551 region-weeks), mortality attaches to **crossing** the threshold,
   not to its duration (step-shaped dose–response); the association strengthens when dry-bulb
   tropical nights are controlled. Because risk follows exposure extent rather than intensity,
   intensity-based scaling overstates risk-relevant growth **1.78×**.
4. Two wet-tropics cities (Hong Kong, Singapore) replicate the humidity-only signal sub-annually
   (orthogonalised meta RR/SD = 1.0030, I² = 0%; dry-bulb negative control inverse), and a power
   diagnostic quantifies why tropical evidence has been absent (minimum detectable effects 1.05
   vs 2.45% per °C).
5. Absolute thresholds saturate in the tropics; percentile-based triggers from WMO/WHO guidance
   fail structurally (P95 = 0 in 75.6% of European regions). We provide a validated graded scale
   and quantify implementation uncertainty (u_c = 36–39%, night definition dominant).

**Why NCC.** The paper connects detection–attribution, health attribution and operational
translation within one physiologically anchored metric, and delivers two practice-changing
corrections (1.78× intensity scaling; percentile-trigger failure) plus observation-constrained
projection numbers (42–70 million Europeans newly exposed per +1 °C).
